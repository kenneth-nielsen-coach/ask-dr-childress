The Public Release AI Vitae Analysis Protocol (Childress, 2026) uses three sequential prompts to examine documented professional qualifications in specific clinical domains.
Prompt 1 — Entry Classification
The AI reviews the complete curriculum vitae and classifies each entry according to the type of evidence it provides: education and training received, supervised experience, research conducted, information disseminated, professional service, or clinical practice. It identifies dates, ambiguities, and duplicate entries, creating an organized, auditable inventory.
Prompt 2 — Domain Analysis and Source Ledger
The AI compares documented qualifications against competence markers in two clinical domains: attachment pathology and delusional thought disorders. It identifies qualifying education, training, and experience; documents gaps and uncertainties; and examines whether professional activities are anchored within the family court system or the broader scientific and clinical community.
Prompt 3 — Discussion and Follow-Up
The AI translates the findings into clear, accessible language for parents, attorneys, and other readers. It explains what the CV documents, what it does not document, and what additional information would clarify unresolved questions, without making judgments about the professional’s actual competence.
Together, the three prompts provide an initial AI-assisted documentation screening that informs subsequent human evaluation and professional clarification.

The Public Release AI Vitae Analysis Protocol as a Community Psychology Consumer Empowerment Intervention
The Public Release AI Vitae Analysis Protocol (Childress, 2026) represents an innovative application of Community Psychology principles to empower consumers of mental health services in the family courts—specifically, parents and their attorneys.
The intervention addresses an imbalance in access to specialized professional knowledge between mental health professionals providing court-involved services and the parents whose families are affected by those services.
Traditionally, parents and attorneys have limited ability to independently evaluate whether a psychologist possesses the education, training, and experience necessary to competently assess, diagnose, and treat the clinical conditions involved in a family court matter. Professional credentials, publications, conference presentations, and years of experience may communicate general professional standing without clearly establishing competence in the specific clinical domains relevant to the services being provided.
The AI Vitae Analysis Protocol changes this relationship by placing a structured analytical instrument directly into the hands of consumers.
1. Empowerment through access to specialized knowledge
A central principle of Community Psychology is empowering individuals and communities to exercise greater understanding, participation, and influence over decisions affecting their lives.
The protocol operationalizes this principle by allowing parents and attorneys to use their own AI systems to examine a mental health professional’s curriculum vitae.
Through three sequential prompts, the AI classifies the professional’s documented activities, examines education and training in specified clinical domains, and presents its findings in accessible language.
For the family court application, the protocol identifies two default domains selected by its author:

Diagnostic assessment and treatment of attachment pathology.

Diagnostic assessment and treatment of delusional thought disorders.
Additional clinical domains, including child abuse, complex trauma, family systems, and other conditions, may be incorporated through supplementary domain modules.
The intervention does not require parents to become clinical psychologists. It provides analytical assistance that enables them to identify relevant documented qualifications, recognize missing information, and participate more meaningfully in discussions about professional competence.
2. Shifting the consumer from passive recipient to informed participant
The intervention changes the parent’s relationship to professional authority.
Rather than relying exclusively on a psychologist’s credentials or representations of expertise, parents can independently examine the documented foundations of that expertise.
The parent can ask whether the professional’s CV documents relevant specialized training, supervised clinical experience, assessment methodology, and research.
This is particularly significant in family court proceedings, where psychological opinions may influence consequential decisions concerning parent-child relationships.
The intervention shifts the consumer from passive reliance on professional authority toward informed participation in evaluating the qualifications of professionals whose services affect the family.
It does not transfer clinical authority to the parent or the AI. It strengthens the consumer’s capacity to ask informed questions and seek appropriate professional clarification.
3. Empowering attorneys through improved access to clinical information
Family law attorneys are another important beneficiary.
Attorneys possess specialized legal knowledge but may not have comparable expertise in clinical psychology, developmental psychopathology, attachment assessment, or delusional thought disorders.
The AI Vitae Analysis Protocol provides a structured method for examining the documented clinical preparation of mental health witnesses.
Its findings can inform attorney consultation, professional qualification inquiries, deposition preparation, and cross-examination.
Rather than relying exclusively on general credentials or the number of evaluations performed, attorneys can examine whether the CV documents education and training relevant to the particular clinical opinions being offered.
The protocol therefore supports more informed legal scrutiny without replacing the attorney’s professional judgment or the court’s decisional authority.
4. A two-level model of human–AI collaboration
The intervention preserves a clear division of responsibility.
LEVEL 1
AI documentation screening
Identify documented education, training, supervised experience, research, dissemination, and professional practice. Compare the evidence against selected clinical domains and identify qualifications, documentation gaps, and unresolved questions.
LEVEL 2
Human evaluation and professional clarification
Parents, attorneys, and qualified professionals examine the findings, consider their significance, obtain supplementary documentation, and make decisions within their respective areas of authority.
The AI does not determine whether a psychologist is competent or whether an ethical violation has occurred.
Instead, it supports more effective human inquiry into an existing professional obligation: practicing within the boundaries of competence under APA Ethical Standard 2.01.
The psychologist whose qualifications are examined retains the opportunity to provide additional evidence supporting their preparation.
5. Potential systems-level effects
Community Psychology is concerned not only with individual empowerment but also with the organizational and institutional environments affecting individuals and families.
The Public Release AI Vitae Analysis Protocol may influence these environments by increasing the visibility of domain-specific professional qualifications.
As consumers and attorneys become better equipped to examine competence-related documentation, mental health professionals may experience greater incentives to describe their relevant training accurately and comprehensively.
Where documentation gaps reflect actual educational gaps, professionals may seek additional training or consultation.
Professional organizations may likewise face greater incentives to identify the clinical competencies associated with specific services and develop appropriate educational opportunities.

Consumers gain analytical access

6. A proof of concept extending beyond the family courts
Although developed for family court applications, the protocol’s underlying methodology is transferable to other areas of mental healthcare.
Domain-specific modules could be developed for autism, ADHD, depression, anxiety disorders, complex trauma, eating disorders, and marital or family therapy.
Consumers could then examine a professional’s documented preparation in the particular domain for which they are seeking services.
In this way, the project illustrates a broader possibility for AI-assisted consumer empowerment throughout the mental healthcare system.
Community Psychology significance
The central innovation of the Public Release AI Vitae Analysis Protocol is not that artificial intelligence makes professional competence judgments.
It is that artificial intelligence makes specialized professional information more accessible, understandable, and examinable by the people whose lives are affected by professional decisions.
The intervention operates at three interconnected levels: individual empowerment through access to information, community empowerment through shared analytical capability, and potential institutional change through increased transparency and accountability.
It is a proof-of-concept Community Psychology intervention that uses emerging AI capabilities to redistribute access to specialized analysis while preserving human professional responsibility and decisional authority.
The fundamental shift is from consumers depending exclusively on professional representations of competence toward consumers possessing an independent means of examining the documented evidence supporting those representations.
In Community Psychology terms, the intervention seeks to increase consumer agency by changing access to the informational resources necessary for meaningful participation in decisions affecting their families.
Its effectiveness remains to be empirically evaluated, but its proposed mechanism is clear: rather than making decisions for parents and attorneys, AI expands their capacity to participate intelligently in decisions that remain theirs to make.
