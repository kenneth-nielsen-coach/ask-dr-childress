2.01 Boundaries of Competence 
(a) Psychologists provide services, teach, and conduct research with populations and in areas only within the boundaries of their competence, based on their education, training, supervised experience, consultation, study, or professional experience.
Cascading Ethical Violations
The violation of Standard 2.01 then leads to a cascading set of ethical violations to Standards 2.04 Bases for Scientific and Professional Judgments, 9.01 Bases for Assessment, and 3.04 Avoiding Harm.
Standard 2.04 Violation
If the psychologist does not know the necessary knowledge required for competence, a violation of Standard 2.01, then they do not apply the necessary knowledge as the basis for their professional judgments, in violation to standard 2.04 Bases for Scientific and Professional Judgments.

2.04 Bases for Scientific and Professional Judgments
Psychologists’ work is based upon established scientific and professional knowledge of the discipline.
Standard 9.01 Violation
If the psychologist does not know the necessary knowledge (a violation to Standard 2.01) and does not apply the necessary knowledge (a violation to Standard 2.04), then their opinions contained in their recommendations, reports, and diagnostic or evaluative statements, including their forensic testimony, are NOT based on information sufficient to substantiate their findings, in violation of Standard 9.01 Bases for Assessment.

9.01 Bases for Assessments 
(a) Psychologists base the opinions contained in their recommendations, reports, and diagnostic or evaluative statements, including forensic testimony, on information and techniques sufficient to substantiate their findings. (See also Standard 2.04, Bases for Scientific and Professional Judgments.)
If the psychologists do not know and apply the necessary knowledge (violations to Standards 2.01 & 2.04), resulting in their opinions being insufficiently substantiated (violation to Standard 9.01), this creates avoidable harm from misdiagnosis, in violation of Standard 3.04 Avoiding Harm.

3.04 Avoiding Harm
(a) Psychologists take reasonable steps to avoid harming their clients/patients, students, supervisees, research participants, organizational clients, and others with whom they work, and to minimize harm where it is foreseeable and unavoidable.  
The forensic custody evaluators in the family courts are in violation of Standards 2.01, 2.04, 9.01, and 3.04 of the APA Ethics Code. The foundational violation is to Standard 2.01 Boundaries of Competence, they don’t know the established scientific and professional knowledge of the discipline necessary for competence.
