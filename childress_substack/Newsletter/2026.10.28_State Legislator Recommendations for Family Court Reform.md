Four recommendations for state legislators regarding family court reform.

State Legislature Family Court Reform
A parent-advocate with her state legislature recently asked me for my “bullet point” recommendations for her state legislators regarding family court reform.
As I do with everything nowadays, I generated an AI Prompt-Response Log response to her consultation request for recommendations. I have four.

Moratorium on Forensic Custody Evaluations: end forensic custody evaluations (“dangerous” & “harmful to children”; NY Blue-Ribbon Commission) until further review is formally conducted, and require DSM-5 diagnoses in all family court custody conflict matters.

APA & OHRP Review: request a formal IRB-like review of forensic custody evaluations, request a risk-benefit analysis when compared to standard clinical diagnostic assessment, and request recommendations for what special protections are warranted for a vulnerable population of parents and children in the family courts.

Licensing Board AI-Vitae Competence: the state licensing boards should request the curriculum vitae (CV) of court-involved forensic custody evaluators and therapists to conduct an AI-augmented vitae analysis for competence (Standard 2.01) in required domains of established knowledge (Standard 2.04).

Pilot Program for the Family Courts: the state legislature can provide a grant to selected universities to develop the diagnostic assessment and treatment protocol for the family courts, along with the legal arguments and court orders to support a treatment-based approach to family custody conflict.

I like a rebuttable presumption of 50-50 shared parenting.

In the absence of child abuse, parents have the right to parent according to their cultural values, their personal values, and their religious values.

In the absence of child abuse, each parent should have as much time and involvement with the child as possible.

In the absence of child abuse, to restrict either parent’s time and involvement with the child would damage the child’s attachment bond to that parent, thereby harming the child and harming that parent.
