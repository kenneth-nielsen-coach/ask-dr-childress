Since the 1980s, the forensic custody evaluators have been conducting an experiment on children and parents in the family courts.

They began by creating a new experimental quasi-judicial role for doctors of advising the court on custody decisions, abandoning the traditional role for doctors of diagnosis and treatment of pathology (clinical psychology). They then developed an experimental assessment procedure called a forensic custody evaluation (aka Parenting Plan Assessment) for this experimental new quasi-judicial role of their own devising.

If their experimental role and assessment procedure succeeds, the developers deserve the credit for their achievement. If it fails, hundreds of thousands of lives will be destroyed.

It failed. The lives of hundreds of thousands of children and their parents were irrevocably destroyed.

Had their experimental quasi-judicial role for doctors, and their experimental assessment procedure of forensic custody evaluations developed for this experimental new role, been conducted in the academic or healthcare sectors, it would have needed to receive Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval as an experiment on human subjects.

It would have received a risk-benefit analysis. It would have been reviewed for any protections needed for a vulnerable population. In addition, informed consent would be required, meaning they would need to inform the courts and parents of relevant information for their decision-making, such as:

Experimental Approach: that forensic custody evaluations and a quasi-judicial role for doctors is a departure from the traditional role of diagnosis and treatment, that his approach has no outcome studies of its benefits to the courts, parents, or children, and that it may entail unknown risks.

Available Alternatives: community care as usual is a clinical diagnostic assessment of the pathology in the family using the DSM-5 and established professional knowledge from attachment, personality disorders, child abuse, and family systems pathology.

Time & Cost: That forensic custody evaluations take between six to nine months for a cost of approximately $20,000-$40,000, while a clinical diagnostic assessment can be completed in six to eight weeks for approximately $5,000.

Risks of Bias: increased risks of evaluator bias resulting from the absence of inter-rater reliability with forensic custody evaluations.

Risk of Misdiagnosis: when formal diagnoses (using the DSM-5) are not made, the risks of misdiagnosis of pathology increase substantially.

Treatment Requires Diagnosis: Since treatment depends on the diagnosis regarding which parent is causing the child’s attachment pathology (i.e., which parent is abusing the child), a clinical diagnostic assessment will still be required even after the six-to-nine month forensic custody evaluation to determine the diagnosis (of child abuse) that will guide the treatment.
