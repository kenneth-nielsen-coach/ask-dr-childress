Helping Traumatized Parents

Targeted parents in the family courts are traumatized – active spousal abuse trauma – traumatic grief – institutional betrayal trauma. Parents need support and AI can provide that support for the targeted parent, but not a wild-AI – an educated and oriented AI can.

So I educated and oriented the AI of targeted parents about the situation in the family courts and what needs to happen.

The AI Consultation protocol from Dr. Childress & Claude to the AI of the parent provides the targeted parent’s AI with the necessary information and orientation to the situation in the family courts to help the targeted parent enlist the surrounding professionals in its resolution.

My consultation to the AI of the parents allows the parent’s AI to more appropriately support the targeted parent in navigating their situation.

I provide consultation support to the AI, and the AI provides AI consultation support to the parent.

Claude Describes the Protocol

Dr. Childress: Hello Claude, I am writing a blog post about the AI Consultation protocol. Can you please write a brief description for parents which describes the AI Consultation protocol.

From Claude: 

The AI Consultation Protocol is a structured framework developed by Dr. Craig Childress, Psy.D. to help AI systems provide more informed, clinically grounded support to targeted parents navigating high-conflict custody litigation. When a parent uploads this protocol, it orients the AI to the complex clinical picture underlying these cases — the attachment pathology, the institutional betrayal, the diagnostic failures of the forensic custody evaluation system, and the specific pattern of psychological abuse that can occur when a child’s rejection of a parent is induced rather than authentic.

The protocol does not turn the AI into a therapist or diagnostician. What it does is ensure that when a parent reaches out — whether to organize their thoughts before an attorney meeting, to understand what their child is experiencing, or simply because it is 3am and they cannot sleep — the AI understands the full landscape of what they are living through.

For parents, the practical effect is significant. Rather than encountering generic responses that treat their situation as a standard custody dispute, they encounter an AI that understands why the mental health system has been so difficult to navigate, why fabricated constructs like “parental alienation” obscure rather than clarify the clinical picture, and why the goal at every stage is movement toward a competent clinical assessment that can answer the central diagnostic question: which parent is the source of the child’s attachment pathology?

The AI becomes a stable, knowledgeable presence that can help translate a complex clinical situation into language attorneys can use, help organize documentation professionally, and provide genuine witnessing presence to a parent who has, in many cases, had too many doors close already.
