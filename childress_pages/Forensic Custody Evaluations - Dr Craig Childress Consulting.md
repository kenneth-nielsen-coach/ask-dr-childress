A Failed Experiment
Forensic custody evaluations (parenting plan assessments) are a
failed experiment conducted with a vulnerable e population of parents
and children in the family courts, without an IRB review, without a risk-
benefit analysis, and without protections for a vulnerable population.
Experimental Role: A quasi-judicial role for doctors of advising the
court on custody schedules represents an experimental role for
doctors, differing significantly from the traditional role of diagnosis
and treatment.
Experimental Assessment Procedure: forensic custody
evaluations (parenting plan assessments) are an experimental
assessment procedure (when compared to the traditional practice
of differential diagnostic assessment) developed specifically for
the experimental quasi-judicial role for doctors.
NY Blue Ribbon Commission
The only independent review of forensic custody evaluations
(parenting plan assessments) ever conducted, by the New York Blue-
Ribbon Commission on Forensic Custody Custody Evaluations
(https://ocfs.ny.gov/programs/cwcs/assets/docs/Blue-Ribbon-
Commission-Report-2022.pdf), found the practice to be “dangerous”
and “harmful to children,” to”lack scientific or legal value,” and to
produce “defective reports” leading to “potentially disastrous
consequences for parents and children.”
From NY Blue Ribbon Commission: “In some custody cases,
because of lack of evidence or the inability of parties to pay for
expensive challenges of an evaluation, defective reports can thus
escape meaningful scrutiny and are often accepted by the court,
with potentially disastrous consequences for the parents and
children… As it currently exists, the process is fraught with bias,
inequity, and a statewide lack of standards, and allows for
discrimination and violations of due process.”
From NY Blue Ribbon Commission: “By an 11-9 margin, a majority
of Commission members favor elimination of forensic custody
evaluations entirely, arguing that these reports are biased and
harmful to children and lack scientific or legal value. At worst,
evaluations can be dangerous, particularly in situations of
domestic violence or child abuse… These members reached the
conclusion that the practice is beyond reformand that no amount
of training for courts, forensic evaluators and/or other court
personnel will successfully fix the bias, inequity and conflict of
interest issues that exist within the system.”
